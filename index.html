When a Tooth Needs to Come Out

A tooth needs to come out when it is too hurt to save, when it is cracked deep below the gum, when it crowds the teeth around it, or when gum trouble has loosened it so much it cannot stay. Wisdom teeth often need to come out when they grow in at a bad angle, or when there is no room for them in the jaw. Sometimes a tooth needs to come out to make room for braces or for a denture that fits better. The team does not take a tooth out just because it hurts a little. They check it with an X-ray first, talk about a root canal or a crown as a way to save it, and only talk about removal when saving it will not work. Simple Removal or Surgical Removal?

A simple removal is for a tooth you can see above the gum, with roots that are not too curved. The team numbs the area, loosens the tooth gently, and lifts it out. A surgical removal is for a tooth that is broken at the gum line, or a wisdom tooth that is still under the gum or bone. That visit takes longer, the team may need to lift the gum a little or take the tooth out in pieces, and they will talk about healing time up front. Both kinds use good numbing, and both end with gauze to press on the socket and stop the bleeding. The team will tell you which kind your tooth needs, before they start.

What Happens on the Day

On the day, you arrive, fill out a short health form, and talk about any medicines you take, since some can affect bleeding. The team takes an X-ray, numbs the tooth and the gum around it, and checks that you are numb before they start. The removal itself often takes a few minutes for a simple tooth, and longer for a wisdom tooth. You will feel pressure, not sharp pain, and you can raise a hand if you need a break. After the tooth is out, the team places gauze on the socket, talks about pain care, and tells you what to eat and what to skip. A Tooth extraction dentist Palm Beach Gardens team member will also tell you when to come back for a check, and what signs mean you should call sooner.

Healing at Home: The First Week

The first day is the most important. Bite on the gauze as the team tells you, rest, and do not rinse hard, spit hard, or use a straw, since that can pull the clot out of the socket and cause a dry socket, which hurts a lot. Eat soft foods like soup, eggs, yogurt, and pasta, and do not smoke or drink through a straw for a few days. A cold pack on the cheek can help swelling, and the pain care the team talks about can help the ache. By the second or third day, the soreness should start to fade. If pain gets worse after a few days, or you taste something bad, call the office. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a tooth extraction hurt?

Most people say the visit itself is calm, because the tooth and gum are numbed well. You may feel sore for a few days after, like a bruise, and the team will tell you what pain care helps most.

How long does it take to heal after a tooth is taken out?

The socket starts to heal in a few days, and the gum closes over it in a week or two. The bone below takes longer to fill in. Follow the home care steps, and the soreness should fade a little each day.

What is a dry socket, and how do I avoid one?

A dry socket is when the clot in the socket comes out too soon, leaving bone bare and sore. Avoid it by not rinsing hard, spitting hard, or using a straw for a few days, and by not smoking. If pain gets worse after a few days, call the office.

Can I eat after a tooth extraction?

Stick to soft foods for a day or two, such as soup, eggs, yogurt, and pasta, and do not chew on the side where the tooth came out. Skip very hot foods while the numb feeling is still there, so you do not bite your cheek.

Do I need a ride home after a wisdom tooth removal?

For a simple removal, you can often drive yourself home. For a surgical wisdom tooth removal, or if you feel woozy, bring someone with you to drive. The team will tell you what to expect for your visit.

What can replace the tooth after it is out?

An implant, a bridge, or a partial denture can fill the gap, based on your mouth, your bite, and your budget. The team will talk about the choices at a follow-up visit, once the socket has healed a bit.

When should I call the office after an extraction?

Call (561) 260-5575 if bleeding will not stop after a few hours, if pain gets worse instead of better, if swelling grows, or if you have a fever. The team would rather hear from you early than have a small issue grow.
